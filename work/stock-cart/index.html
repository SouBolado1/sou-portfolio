0-Fee Stock Cart Challenge

A two-round stock picking game timed to Walmart’s and NVIDIA’s earnings, where every cart became a shareable card and an entry on a live ROI leaderboard.

02

Objectives & strategic goals

Put MEXC’s 0-fee RealStocks lineup in front of traders during 0808 Stock Season, MEXC’s annual celebration of 0 fees and infinite opportunities.

Drive user-generated content on X by turning stock picking into a game, timed to two major earnings prints.

Pillar 01

Cart builder website

Built with AP Collective: users pick five 0-fee stocks from the week’s pool and split 100% across them.

Pillar 02

Shareable cart card

Every cart generates a personalised MEXC 0808 card, posted on X and verified as the user’s entry.

Pillar 03

ROI leaderboard & prizes

Carts are ranked on ROI over the trading week. The top 3 each round win USDT.

03

Two rounds, two earnings prints

The campaign ran over two weeks, each built around a stock pool tied to a major earnings report.

Round 1 · Aug 17 – 21

Retail Cart Challenge

Built around Walmart’s earnings, a read on U.S. consumer spending, retail and e-commerce.

Round 2 · Aug 24 – 28

AI Cart Challenge

Built around NVIDIA’s earnings, which move memory, data centre and cloud names alongside it.

04

How it works

From a pool of stocks to a verified entry on X in four steps.

1

Enter your X handle

Pulls the user’s name and picture from X for their card.

2

Fill your cart

Pick five stocks from the week’s pool and split 100% across them (min 5% each).

3

Post your cart card

Generate the card and share it on X, tagging MEXC.

4

Submit to verify

Paste the tweet link to lock the entry before Wednesday’s close.

Campaign branding

MEXC | 0808, #MEXC0808 and hosted by AP Collective.

06

Why it worked

Timed to the market’s big moments

Anchoring each round to a major earnings print gave participants a real reason to have an opinion that week.

A no-capital way to play

No capital, position size or leverage. Anyone could join in and show off their market read, which widened the pool of participants.

A cart is a take

Each card was a public call on the market. A live leaderboard kept people checking back and posting about their rank.
